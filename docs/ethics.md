# Ethics and Limitations

## Not for clinical use

MedDialBench evaluates diagnostic *robustness* of language models under
adversarial patient behaviors. The cases, dialogues, and model outputs in
this benchmark must not be used to:

- Inform patient care or clinical decisions
- Train or validate any system intended for deployment in a healthcare setting
- Support medical advice, telemedicine, or triage

The patient simulator is an adversarial research artifact. Its behaviors
(fabrication, contradiction, withholding, impatience, etc.) are designed to
probe model failure modes and do not reflect how real patients should be
treated.

## Data provenance

- **Clinical vignettes** are derived from the public MedQA dataset (USMLE /
  Mainland China medical licensing exams) and rewritten into colloquial
  patient language. They contain no real patient identifiers.
- **Dialogues** were generated by Claude Opus 4.5 acting as the patient and
  by five evaluated doctor models. No human patients are involved at any
  point in the pipeline.
- **Ground-truth diagnoses** come from MedQA answer keys.

## Dual-use considerations

The benchmark measures how much adversarial patient behavior degrades model
accuracy. Publishing this could, in principle, inform adversarial prompting
against deployed clinical assistants. We judge the transparency value for
safety research to outweigh the marginal offensive utility — the failure
modes we report (e.g., fabricated symptoms derailing diagnosis) are already
well known to practitioners, and measuring them is a prerequisite to
mitigating them.

## Model version drift

LLM providers retire and update models without notice. Results from
re-running this benchmark against newer model snapshots will not match the
paper's numbers exactly. The HuggingFace dialogues dataset preserves the
original runs for longitudinal comparison.

## Limitations explicitly acknowledged

- 85 cases is small by general-NLP standards; the paper appendix includes a
  power analysis justifying this for the paper's main claims.
- A single patient model (Claude Opus 4.5) was used throughout. Cross-model
  patient validation is future work.
- Behavioral dimensions are held fixed within a dialogue (no dynamic
  trajectories).
- Judge agreement on the "misled" label is substantially lower than on
  "correct" (kappa = 0.469 vs 0.882); the misled metric is therefore
  exploratory.
